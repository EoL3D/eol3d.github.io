About the dataset

EoL3D is a dataset of 66 end-of-life desktop computers, each disassembled by hand, one step at a time. After every step the machine is recorded from 4 fixed views. Instance masks, part states, removal relations, question-answer pairs, operator video and robot demonstrations are built on those records.

  • 66 66 end-of-life desktops
  • 2,748 2,748 disassembly steps
  • 84,225 84,225 instance annotations
  • 184,263 184,263 question-answer pairs

Updates

  1. Nov 20, 2026

How it was recorded

  1. One step at a time

    An operator disassembles the machine by hand. Every step is logged: the action, the part and the tool.

  2. Four views after every step

    The scanner and three cameras record what is left on the bench, so all four views show the same state.

  3. Annotated backward

    Annotators start from the late steps: a part that is exposed there is followed back through the frames where it was covered, under one identity.

  4. Questions, video and robot data

    Question-answer pairs are generated from the annotations and the log. Two video cameras film the operator's hands, and a robot arm repeats part removals under teleoperation.

What is in the dataset

Each of the 66 desktops is recorded after every step of its disassembly and annotated part by part. These are the parts of the release, shown with the dataset's own pictures.
