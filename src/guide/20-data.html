One step, four views, the log

Step 1 is the machine before any action. After every later step, the 3D scanner looking straight down and three cameras record what is left: two OAK cameras, one nearly overhead and one at an angle, and a RealSense camera at an angle. The operation log gives each step its name and its actions: verb, part and tool.

Scanner
raw/scanner/<batch>/<batch>/<NN>/RGB<n>1/: a burst of colour images per step, P_0.png, P_1.png, …
OAK
raw/oak/Desktop NN/Disassemble/Camera_1|2/<step>/: colour JPEG, colour aligned to depth (PNG), raw depth (.npy, .png), point cloud (.ply)
RealSense
raw/realsense/Desktop NN/Disassemble/<step>/ (the folder is named Disassembly for desktops 18 to 22): colour, raw depth (.npy), point cloud (.ply)
Calibration
Calibration/ in each desktop folder of the cameras
Log
annotations/scanner/D<nn>/sequence.json: per step its name, type and actions; verbs: unscrew, disconnect, open, release, remove, displace, reorient
{"step": 10, "name": "Open Power Module",
 "actions": [{"verb": "displace", "target": "psu.01", "tool": "none"}]}

Read it right Every picture of a step is taken after that step's action: on S05 the part removed at step 5 is already gone. Of each scanner burst, frames.json names the image the masks were drawn on.

Instance masks

Every scanner frame carries an instance mask for every part it shows, in {{ n.classes | num }} classes, and every frame was confirmed by a human annotator: {{ n.scanner_frames | num }} frames, {{ n.parts | num }} parts, {{ n.annotations | num }} annotations.

Folder
annotations/scanner/D<nn>/, one per desktop
Pictures
images/S<nn>.png: the scanner picture of step <nn>, the raw file byte for byte
Label maps
labels/S<nn>.png: 16-bit instance ids, 0 = background
COCO
annotations.json: segmentation (visible mask, RLE), amodal_segmentation, amodal_bbox, visibility, occlusion_ratio, occluders, clean, iscrowd, attributes (instance key, state, placement)
Per desktop
frames.json (per step), instances.json (per part), sequence.json (steps and state timeline)

Read it right Take pixels from the label map, not from segmentation: where two confirmed masks overlap, the map gives each pixel to one part. instance_id holds within one desktop; join on key.

Visible region and complete extent

Parts cover each other. Each annotation holds two masks: the region the scanner sees, and the complete extent of the part, including what other parts cover. It also says how much is hidden and by which parts.

Visible
segmentation, bbox, area
Complete extent
amodal_segmentation, amodal_bbox, amodal_area
Hidden
occlusion_ratio (share of the complete extent hidden), occluders ([{"id", "px"}], the parts that hide it)
Label
visibility: visible, visible_tiny, too_small, occluded_partial, occluded_full; clean: not covered at all

Read it right A part labelled visible can still be partly covered; clean is the test for “not covered”. A crowd row (iscrowd: 1) is a part in place but fully covered: empty visible mask, complete extent kept.

Part states

Every part has a state and a placement at every step, so a part can be followed through the whole disassembly, up to the step it leaves the case.

Per step
instances.json: states["<step>"] = state and placement; leaving: the step the part leaves, and the frames before and after
Timeline
sequence.json: timeline, runs of (from, to, state, placement) per part
States
closed, displaced, fastened, installed, loosened, open, plugged, present, removed, unplugged
Placements
in_chassis, on_bench

Relations and removal order

Relations between parts (connected, fastened, covered, locked, blocked) say what has to come off before a part is free. With the states, they give the removal order. Before the CPU of this desktop could come out:

  1. 1Cover covers the CPU fan
  2. 2–5Cooler screws fasten the fan
  3. 6Fan connector is unplugged
  4. 7CPU fan covers the socket lever
  5. 8Socket lever locks the CPU
  6. GoalCPU is free to come out
Per part
instances.json: parent, attached, mounted_on, fastens, socket_host, captive, attrs.cable_owner
Graph
vlm/vlmpack_2026-11-05/data/vlm_<unit>.oracle.jsonl, the lines of "type": "desktop": edges, groups, instances, log
Edge types
connected_to, fastened_by, covered_by, locked_by, blocked_by

Read it right An edge reads from the part that waits: {"target": "cpu.01", "type": "locked_by", "blocker": "cpu_socket_lever.01"} means the lever is dealt with before the CPU.

Component pictures

After disassembly, the removed parts were laid out on the bench and photographed, and each part was masked on its own, so a part can be recognised outside the machine too: {{ n.component_shots | num }} shots, {{ n.component_annotations | num }} annotations.

Folder
annotations/components/<view>/D<nn>/, views cmp_scan, cmp_oak1, cmp_oak2, cmp_rs
Files
images/C<shot>, labels/C<shot>.png, annotations.json (COCO), instances.json
Join
the same instance_id and key as the scanner annotations of that desktop
Labels
placement on_bench, state removed; in_sequence: false for a part only the bench shows

3D

The scanner measures depth at most of its pixels, so most of each visible mask is also a set of measured 3D points, in millimetres. Each annotated step has its reconstruction, named like its picture, and every point carries the instance and the class of the part it lies on. Nothing is filled in: a surface the scanner could not see has no points.

Scanner
reconstructions/scanner/D<NN>/S<nn>.ply: x y z in millimetres in the scanner camera frame, red green blue, instance (the value of labels/S<nn>.png at the point's pixel, 0 = none) and category (the COCO class id); format, pixel mapping and checksums in its README.md and manifest.csv, which lists every annotated step.
Parts
select a part's points with instance, or use annotations/scanner/tools/parts3d.py, which cuts each part out of a reconstruction with the masks; steps in HOW_TO_EXTRACT_3D_PARTS.md, route and best frame per part in parts_3d_plan.csv
Cameras
Depth and point clouds of the OAK and RealSense cameras are in raw/ (.npy, .ply)

Read it right The complete extent is a silhouette on the image, not a set of points: the hidden surface of a part was never measured.

Question-answer pairs

{{ n.qa_pairs | num }} records in {{ n.qa_tasks | num }} tasks are generated from the masks, states, relations and log. A record keeps what a model is shown (prompt) apart from the truth (label). {{ n.qa_eval | num }} records form the evaluation subset; the tasks and how they are scored are under Benchmark.

Read it right Show a model the prompt only. images/ holds the frames of the evaluation subset; for any other record, the image manifest gives desktop, view and step of each frame.

Records
data/vlm_<unit>.jsonl, units scan, oak1, oak2, multi; one JSON record per line
Prompt
task, images (opaque ids), question, options, marks
Label
answer, evidence, rationale, desktop, step, split
Frames
data/vlm_<unit>.images_manifest.jsonl (id to file), images/<view>/D<nn>/s<nnn>
Subset
eval_v3/: the evaluation records per unit
Reasoning
reasoning/<unit>.jsonl: question, answer and the reasoning in words, per record, for training

  • PerceptionD19 / Step 03

    Question
    Count the cpu cooler screws that are still in place in this image.
    Answer
    {"count": 3}
    Reasoning
    The cpu cooler screws still in place are counted on the machine as it stands (a screw taken out leaves an empty hole): 3. So the count is 3.

  • ChangeD56 / Steps 12–13, before and after

    Question
    Do the pictures before and after show the same machine state, or did something change?
    Answer
    {"changed": true, "events": [{"target": "SSD storage drive", "old": "installed", "new": "removed"}]}
    Reasoning
    Comparing the two pictures, the SSD storage drive changed between the two moments. So yes, the machine changed.

  • PlanningD34 / Step 24

    Question
    Looking at the machine in these pictures, which one of the following actions is possible right now?
    • take out the RAM module
    • take out the motherboard
    • take out the CPU
    Answer
    {"verb": "remove", "target": "RAM module"}
    Reasoning
    Take out the RAM module: both its RAM latches are open, so the RAM module can be taken out. Not yet take out the CPU: the CPU socket lever still locks the CPU. Not yet take out the motherboard: the CPU and the RAM module are still mounted on the motherboard and all 6 of its motherboard screws are still in. So the one possible now is: take out the RAM module.

Questions, answers and reasoning are quoted from the records. Only the scanner image is shown here; the change and planning records also show the three cameras of the same moment.

Operator video

Two synchronised video cameras film the bench at an angle from two sides while the operator works. The recordings are cut to the moments of work and paired: {{ n.clips | num }} paired clips, {{ n.clip_hours | num }} hours from {{ n.clip_desktops | num }} desktops, no sound, faces masked. A clip shows the action itself; the scanner frame of the same step shows its result.

Read it right These two video cameras are not the cameras of raw/, which photograph the machine after each step. When the operator looks down, a face mask can overlap the hands.

Folder
video/hand_work_selected/desktop_NN/
Clips
clips/NNNN_video_camera_1.mp4 and clips/NNNN_video_camera_2.mp4, one pair per segment
Montage
video_camera_1.mp4, video_camera_2.mp4: every segment of the desktop in one file per camera
Segments
selected_segments.csv per desktop; all_selected_segments.csv at the top: start and end in each camera's video, duration, the step of the log
Player
index.html plays the two cameras side by side, offline

Robot demonstrations

A robot arm takes single components out in two settings: Fixtured, with parts held in slots on a base plate ({{ n.robot_fixtured | num }} episodes), and On motherboard, with parts still mounted on a board ({{ n.robot_on_board | num }} episodes). A base camera and a wrist camera film each episode, recorded frame by frame with the robot's state. They are a separate recording, not the disassembly sequences above.

Folders
raw/robot/on_fixture/raw_data/<component>/, raw/robot/on_motherboard/data/raw_data/<component>/: one episode_*.zip per episode
Frames
frame_*.pkl: timestamps, joint_positions, tcp_pose, gripper_pos, base_rgb, wrist_rgb
Episode
episode_meta.json, control_trace.jsonl
Catalog
archive_catalog.jsonl: every archive with its component, frames, bytes and SHA-256

Read it right Frames are Python pickles: open them only from this repository, in an isolated environment. The archives are native recordings, not a LeRobot or RLDS conversion.
